What Causes Hair Loss

Doctors call it alopecia — but you probably know it simply as hair loss. And you're not alone. The most common type, androgenetic alopecia (also known as male or female pattern baldness), is caused by a hormone called DHT that slowly shrinks sensitive hair follicles. Over time, each new hair grows back thinner and shorter until it stops growing altogether. Genetics play a big role in how sensitive your follicles are to DHT, but the good news is: there are proven treatments that can slow, stop, or even reverse this process at almost every stage.

Genetics isn't the whole story, though. Chronic stress, poor nutrition, thyroid problems, hormonal imbalances, and autoimmune conditions can all speed up hair thinning or trigger sudden shedding. That's why proper diagnosis matters. At Barley Hospital, our specialists run a thorough assessment — including blood work and scalp imaging — to pinpoint exactly what's causing your hair loss before recommending a treatment plan tailored to you.

Non-Surgical Treatments That Work

PRP (Platelet-Rich Plasma) Therapy

We draw a small amount of your blood, spin it to concentrate the platelets, and inject the nutrient-rich plasma directly into thinning areas. These platelets release growth factors that wake up dormant follicles, improve blood flow, and speed up tissue repair. A full course typically involves 3— sessions spaced a few weeks apart — and most patients notice real improvement in density within a few months.

Topical Minoxidil

This FDA-approved treatment goes directly on the scalp twice a day. It works by boosting blood flow to hair follicles and extending their growth phase. Available over the counter in 2% and 5% strengths, you'll need to stick with it daily — most people see visible thickening after 4— months. Works for both men and women.

Oral Finasteride

This prescription pill blocks the enzyme that converts testosterone into DHT — the hormone responsible for genetic hair loss. By reducing scalp DHT by around 70%, finasteride effectively stops further shedding and often triggers new regrowth, especially at the crown. It's designed for men and needs to be taken consistently to maintain results.

Low-Level Laser Therapy (LLLT)

LLLT uses red light (650— 70nm) to energize hair follicle cells from the outside in. It boosts cellular energy production, improves micro-circulation, and pushes follicles into the growth phase. Completely painless and non-invasive, you can have treatments done at our clinic or use FDA-cleared laser devices at home. It pairs well with other treatments for even better results.

Why Combination Therapy Works Best

Get Better Results by Combining Treatments

Years of clinical experience show one clear pattern: combining treatments always beats going with just one. For example, a hair transplant can rebuild completely bald areas, while medication like finasteride and minoxidil protects the hair you still have. Adding PRP sessions after surgery speeds up healing and helps more grafts survive. And laser therapy creates a healthier scalp environment that makes everything else work even better. At Barley Hospital, we put together a custom plan based on your specific hair loss pattern, how advanced it is, and what you want to achieve. Our own data consistently shows that patients on combination protocols see noticeably better results than those using a single treatment.

Which Treatment Is Right for You

The right treatment depends on a few key factors: what's causing your hair loss, how far it's progressed, your age, overall health, and personal preferences. In the early stages, medication alone can often make a big difference. More advanced cases may need surgery paired with ongoing medical treatment to keep remaining hair healthy. The best place to start is a professional consultation. At Barley Hospital, our clinicians use advanced diagnostic tools — including trichoscopic imaging and detailed scalp mapping — to assess your condition objectively. We then walk you through your options with honest expectations, realistic timelines, and clear pricing. There's no one-size-fits-all solution here. We figure out what works for you.

Common Questions About Hair Loss Treatment

Expert answers to help you make informed decisions

What is the best treatment for early-stage hair thinning
For early-stage thinning, we typically recommend starting with topical minoxidil combined with PRP therapy. This dual approach tackles hair loss from two angles — minoxidil extends the growth phase while PRP's growth factors wake up dormant follicles. Most patients see noticeable improvement within 3-4 months when both treatments are used consistently.
How does PRP hair therapy work
PRP (Platelet-Rich Plasma) therapy involves drawing a small amount of your blood, processing it to concentrate the platelets, and injecting the resulting plasma into your scalp. These platelets release growth factors that promote blood circulation, reduce inflammation, and encourage hair follicles to enter the active growth phase. A typical course involves 3-6 sessions spaced 4-6 weeks apart.
Is PRP therapy painful
The procedure is well-tolerated by most patients. We apply a topical numbing cream before injections to minimize discomfort. Most describe the sensation as mild pressure or slight pinching. Any soreness typically resolves within a day or two. The entire session takes about 45-60 minutes.
What is the difference between minoxidil and finasteride
Minoxidil is a topical solution that works by increasing blood flow to hair follicles and extending their growth phase. Finasteride is an oral medication that blocks DHT, the hormone responsible for genetic hair loss. They work through completely different mechanisms and are often prescribed together for enhanced results. Minoxidil suits both men and women, while finasteride is typically for men only.
Can women use finasteride for hair loss
Finasteride is generally not recommended for women, especially those of childbearing age, due to potential risks during pregnancy. However, post-menopausal women may sometimes be prescribed it under strict medical supervision. We offer alternative treatments for women including spironolactone, topical minoxidil, and PRP therapy.
How long does it take to see results from hair loss treatment
Results vary depending on the treatment and individual factors. Minoxidil typically shows results after 4-6 months of consistent use. PRP therapy may show improvements after 2-3 sessions. Finasteride can slow hair loss within 3-6 months, with regrowth visible after 6-12 months. Early treatment always yields better and faster results.
Are there any side effects from hair loss medications
Minoxidil may cause mild scalp irritation or initial temporary shedding. Finasteride can rarely cause decreased libido or erectile dysfunction in less than 2% of users, which typically resolves upon discontinuation. PRP therapy has minimal side effects since it uses your own blood. Our doctors thoroughly discuss potential side effects and monitor your response throughout treatment.
What is low-level laser therapy for hair
Low-level laser therapy (LLLT) uses specific red light wavelengths (650-670nm) to stimulate cellular activity in hair follicles. This increases ATP production, improves blood circulation, and extends the growth phase. Treatments can be done in-clinic or at home with FDA-cleared devices. It works well as an adjunct to other treatments.
Do I need to stop hair loss treatment after seeing results
Non-surgical treatments like minoxidil and finasteride require ongoing use to maintain results. If you stop, the hair loss process typically resumes. PRP therapy may require maintenance sessions every 6-12 months after the initial series. During your consultation, we'll create a long-term plan that fits your lifestyle and goals.
Can stress cause permanent hair loss
Stress-induced hair loss (telogen effluvium) is usually temporary. Chronic stress can push hair follicles into a resting phase, causing increased shedding. However, once the stress is managed, hair typically regrows within 6-9 months. If stress-related hair loss persists, it may unmask underlying genetic hair loss, which would require targeted treatment.
What tests are done to diagnose hair loss
Our diagnostic process includes a detailed medical history, scalp examination with trichoscopy (magnified imaging of hair follicles), pull test to assess shedding rate, and sometimes blood tests to check for nutritional deficiencies, thyroid function, or hormonal imbalances. This comprehensive approach ensures we identify the exact cause of your hair loss.
Is alopecia areata treatable
Yes, alopecia areata is treatable. This autoimmune condition causing patchy hair loss often responds well to corticosteroid injections, topical immunotherapy, or JAK inhibitors. We also use PRP therapy and minoxidil as supportive treatments. The prognosis is generally good, especially for patients with limited patches.

Can I combine PRP with a hair transplant
Absolutely! PRP is often used alongside hair transplantation to enhance healing and improve graft survival rates. Many patients receive PRP injections during the transplant procedure and in follow-up sessions. This combination can lead to faster recovery, reduced shock loss, and improved overall results.
What is the Norwood scale and how is it used
The Norwood scale is a classification system that measures the severity of male pattern baldness, ranging from Stage 1 (minimal recession) to Stage 7 (extensive hair loss). We use this scale to determine your stage of hair loss, predict future progression, and recommend appropriate treatments. Earlier stages respond better to non-surgical options.
How do I know if my hair loss is genetic or temporary
Genetic hair loss (androgenetic alopecia) follows a predictable pattern — receding hairline or crown thinning in men, diffuse thinning in women. Temporary hair loss from stress, illness, or nutritional deficiency tends to be more sudden and diffuse. Our specialists can tell the difference through examination and testing, so you get the right treatment from day one.

Many treatments like PRP require multiple sessions, which we can coordinate with your travel schedule.
